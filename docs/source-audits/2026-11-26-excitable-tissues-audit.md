# Excitable Tissues MCQ source audit — 26 November 2026

## Scope and method

Reviewed the extractable text of the 100-item *Excitable Tissues* MCQ compilation and compared its keyed answers and wording with standard membrane and action-potential physiology. This is an answer-quality audit, not a reproduction or transcription of the source. The source itself is not modified. The findings below distinguish definite answer-key/content problems from items that need more precise wording.

## Definite corrections or material qualifications

| Source item | Finding | Correct concept / recommended treatment |
|---|---|---|
| 15 and 78 | The keyed description says a neuron can be stimulated only by a “very weak” stimulus in the relative refractory period. | Incorrect. A stronger-than-normal depolarising stimulus is generally needed because some Na+ channels have not recovered and K+ conductance / after-hyperpolarisation may persist. |
| 17 | Says voltage-gated K+ channels begin opening only “when repolarization starts”. | Oversimplified timing. Delayed-rectifier K+ channels activate during depolarisation, with their outward current contributing importantly to repolarisation. |
| 20 | Attributes resting membrane potential primarily to the Na+/K+ pump. | The pump maintains Na+ and K+ gradients and contributes a small electrogenic effect; resting potential is determined mainly by selective membrane permeability, especially K+ conductance, together with ionic gradients. |
| 24 and 65 | Treats Ca2+ as the ion responsible for the depolarisation phase of all cardiac muscle action potentials. | Must specify cell type. Fast-response atrial/ventricular contractile myocytes use fast Na+ current for phase 0; sinoatrial/atrioventricular nodal cells use Ca2+ current for their phase 0 upstroke. L-type Ca2+ current contributes to the contractile-cell plateau. |
| 45 | Says Na+ channels return to their resting state “just after the peak”. | At the peak, many fast Na+ channels inactivate. They recover from inactivation and return to the closed, activatable state as the membrane repolarises; recovery is voltage-dependent, not simply an instantaneous post-peak event. |
| 49 | The keyed option defines threshold attainment, rather than the all-or-none principle itself. | The principle is that a successfully triggered action potential has a stereotyped amplitude for that excitable membrane; stronger suprathreshold stimuli do not produce proportionally larger individual action potentials. Threshold is the trigger condition, not the definition. |
| 63 | Says both Na+ and K+ channels are inactive during the absolute refractory period. | Incorrect. Fast voltage-gated Na+ channels are inactivated and unavailable; voltage-gated K+ channels may be open and contributing to repolarisation. |
| 71 | A stimulus bringing the membrane closer to threshold is keyed as necessarily initiating an action potential. | Overstated. A subthreshold depolarisation increases the probability of reaching threshold, but does not guarantee an action potential unless the net depolarisation reaches threshold. |
| 81 | Uses “refractory period” for a period in which no further action potential can be generated regardless of stimulus. | This describes the absolute refractory period specifically. The relative refractory period permits another action potential with a stronger stimulus. |
| 95 | The keyed answer describes Na+ channel inactivation/K+ channel opening, not precisely the voltage peak. | The peak is the maximum positive membrane potential (overshoot) near the transition from net inward to net outward current; channel-state changes contribute to the transition but are not a precise definition of the peak. |

## Ambiguous or overgeneralised items

| Source item | Issue | Recommended treatment |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | Defines threshold as the potential at which voltage-gated Na+ channels open. | Some Na+ channels open below threshold. Threshold is the point at which regenerative inward current overcomes opposing currents and produces a self-amplifying depolarisation. |
| 11 | Keys the axon hillock as the location of voltage-gated Na+ and K+ channels, while the node of Ranvier is also a valid location. | Ask specifically about the trigger zone / axon initial segment, or about nodes in myelinated axons; avoid a single-answer stem that permits both. |
| 14 | Asks which tissue has a “typically shorter” action potential without defining the comparison. | Duration varies by cell type and preparation. State the exact cell types being compared (e.g., skeletal muscle fibre versus ventricular myocyte). |
| 43 | Says K+ channels “open during repolarization”. | Acceptable as a broad functional summary, but more accurate wording is that delayed K+ channel activation begins during depolarisation and outward K+ current drives repolarisation. |

## Sound items / important distinctions retained

- The absolute refractory period spans depolarisation and much of repolarisation because fast Na+ channels are inactivated (item 9).
- Increased extracellular K+ generally depolarises the resting membrane potential; severe sustained hyperkalaemia can then inactivate Na+ channels and impair excitability (item 26).
- Myelin increases membrane resistance and lowers effective capacitance, enabling saltatory conduction (items 8, 41, 61, 96, 99).
- Presynaptic Ca2+ entry triggers vesicle fusion and neurotransmitter release (items 44, 72, 100).
- The Na+/K+ ATPase maintains transmembrane Na+ and K+ gradients (items 50, 76), but should not be described as the principal immediate generator of resting potential.
